Research Mission

Understanding, preventing and treating neurodegeneration has been the central goal guiding my work from the outset. My pursuits in clinical medicine, neuroscience, functional genomics, AI and engineering are interconnected parts of this long-term mission, rather than separate interests or a change in direction.

I see neurodevelopment as fundamental to understanding neurodegeneration. Studying how neural cells develop, acquire identity and maintain their function can help reveal why they become vulnerable and fail in disease.

  • Experimental neuroscience: Research experience in human cortical progenitor biology, neurodevelopment and CRISPR perturbation, providing a foundation for investigating neuronal identity, cellular vulnerability and neurodegenerative mechanisms.
  • Clinical medicine: Experience in patient assessment, escalation, communication and safe prescribing, grounding my research ambitions in clinical needs and patient outcomes.
  • Genomics and computational biology: CRISPR-screen analysis, RNA sequencing, single-cell transcriptomics and computational approaches to investigate cellular pathways and potential therapeutic targets.
  • AI and engineering: Developing software, AI systems, research tools and hardware solutions to support scientific investigation, analysis and experimentation.

My aim remains to bring these fields together to tackle two fundamental challenges:

  1. Understand why neurons degenerate: Investigate selective neuronal vulnerability, disease mechanisms and molecular pathways using human cellular models, genetics, CRISPR and computational approaches.
  2. Prevent or slow neurodegeneration: Identify and assess therapeutic targets, improve disease models and contribute to the development of disease-modifying treatments, including cell- and gene-based approaches.
