OET WRITING · AI SCORES EXPLAINED

Is ChatGPT's OET score accurate? Short answer: treat it as noise.

If you pasted a practice letter into ChatGPT and got a number back, you probably want to know whether that number means anything. Here is what the evidence says, why the gap exists, and what AI feedback is genuinely useful for.

The experiment that settles it

A candidate on the r/OETforECFMG subreddit pasted a practice referral letter into ChatGPT and received 300. Suspicious of the number, they then pasted the official OET answer key for the same task — a model answer, the strongest possible letter — and ChatGPT scored it 380/500.

"…gave me (the answer key) 380/500" — the candidate, describing the result (source thread)

An eighty-point spread between a real letter and a model answer — produced by the same scorer, on the same task — means the number carries almost no signal about the letter. If a tool scores a perfect response 380, it cannot be trusted to tell you whether your 300-letter is a 250 or a 340. The only defensible conclusion: do not make decisions (remark, retake, study plan) based on a chatbot's OET score.

Why chatbots can't reproduce OET marking

  • Calibration. Real OET writing scores come from trained assessors who mark against the six published criteria, with the sub-test double-marked by two assessors who don't see each other's scores, and discrepancies escalated to a senior assessor. A general-purpose chatbot has none of that calibration.
  • No rubric anchoring. The model produces a plausible-sounding number because it has seen score-like numbers in training data — not because it measured your letter against level descriptors.
  • Task-specific judgement. Selection — which case notes belong in the letter — depends on the specific task notes. Generative models regularly praise letters that selected the wrong content, or penalise correct omissions.

What AI feedback IS good for

The failure above is about scoring — producing a number. Structured feedback is a different job, and AI can genuinely help with it: breaking your letter down criterion by criterion, showing which sections are thin, flagging register problems ("do an endoscopy" → "for endoscopic assessment"), and comparing your organisation against the expected letter shape. That is the difference between "you got 300" (noise) and "your purpose line is clear but your request is buried in the last paragraph" (actionable).

Self-mark checklist: the six OET writing criteria

CriterionThe one question to ask of your letter
PurposeIs the reason for writing unmistakable in the first two lines?
ContentDid I expand only what the task notes asked for — nothing invented, nothing important missing?
Conciseness & ClarityIs every sentence pulling weight at 180–200 words?
Genre & StyleDoes it read like one clinician writing to another — not a text message, not an essay?
Organisation & LayoutDoes each paragraph carry one movement (history → findings → management → request)?
LanguageAre my tense slips and register errors the kind I repeat, or one-offs?
