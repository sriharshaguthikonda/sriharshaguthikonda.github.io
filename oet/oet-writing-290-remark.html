OET writing 290: what a remark can and cannot fix

You scored 290 in writing. You prepared harder, retook it — and got 290 again. Now the remark feels like the only lever left. Here is what a remark actually re-scores, and what to do instead while you wait for it.

The pattern is real, and you're not imagining it

A candidate on r/OETforECFMG described exactly this: a first attempt at 290, then months of preparation — criteria checked repeatedly, 20 days of practice letters — and a second attempt at 290 again, with an A in listening and reading both times.

"I decided to remark thinking they maybe made a mistake. The remark did not change my marks at all." — the candidate, writing after the second result (source thread)

It feels unfair, and the frustration is legitimate. But the repeated number carries information: the same performance keeps earning the same score, twice, from independent marking.

What a remark actually is

A remark re-scores the same performance. It exists to catch marking variance — a borderline letter scored too harshly by one assessor, or an administrative error. It does not re-read your mind, your intention, or your preparation. So the honest question is not "will they realise I deserved more?" but: is my letter genuinely borderline, or is it a solid C+ that was marked correctly?

  • A remark can fix: a genuinely borderline letter scored below its level; an irregularity during the sub-test that affected your performance and was officially noted.
  • A remark cannot fix: a skill gap. If the letter systematically loses marks for the same reasons — thin purpose line, wrong case-note selection, over-length, register slips — a second assessor will see the same letter the first one saw.

The observed pattern — 290 holding at 290 after a remark — is consistent with the second case: a stable score usually means the marking is reproducing itself, not that it's broken.

What actually moves a stuck writing score

A stuck 290 is almost never "write more letters". It's a specific criterion capping the letter, and until you know which one, more volume just rehearses the gap. The work that moves the score:

If the weak spot is…The fix looks like…
Case-note selection (Content)Expanding only what the task notes justify — the most common 290 pattern is relevant-but-irrelevant detail added to look thorough
Word count (Conciseness)Hard discipline at 180–200 words; padding never buys back missing content
PurposeThe request unmistakable in the first two lines, not buried in the last paragraph
Register (Genre & Style)One clinician to another — "for endoscopic assessment", not "do an endoscopy"
LanguageYour repeated slips, listed and drilled — not general grammar study

So should you pay for the remark?

Reasonable when the score genuinely contradicts strong independent feedback on the same letter — for example, structured per-criterion reviews that consistently place the letter above the score it received, or a documented technical disruption during the sub-test. Not reasonable as a hedge against a diagnosed skill gap: it costs money and changes nothing about the next attempt. Before paying, get the letter diagnosed criterion by criterion — by a tutor, or by structured feedback rather than a chatbot's single number (see why AI scores can't be trusted).
