Is ChatGPT enough to assess your OET writing?

No tutor, no study group, and a chatbot that will happily grade your letter. A candidate on r/OETforECFMG asked it plainly: "Is giving a prompt to ChatGPT enough?" The honest answer: it's better than nothing — and not enough. Here's the gap, and the loop that closes it.

Why the raw prompt under-delivers

Ask a general chatbot "score my OET letter" and you get an impressionistic reply: fluent, encouraging, unanchored. Three specific problems:

  • No calibration. The model wasn't trained to reproduce OET marking. One candidate proved the point: their practice letter scored 300 — and the official model answer for the same task scored 380/500 from the same chatbot. A scorer that penalises a model answer cannot rank your letter meaningfully.
  • Prompt-sensitive numbers. The same letter gets different numbers depending on how you ask. A score that moves when your phrasing does isn't measuring your writing.
  • Feedback without structure. "Good job, watch your grammar" is not feedback you can act on. OET writing is marked on six named criteria — feedback that isn't organised around them can't tell you where marks are lost.
"To those who have passed OET, how do you assess your writing? Is giving a prompt to ChatGPT enough? I'm not sure about my writing, and I have no one to ask." — the question that prompted this page (source thread)

What "enough" looks like

The difference between useless and useful AI feedback is structure, not honesty or effort. Feedback is actionable when it is:

Structured feedback does…A raw chat prompt does…
Scores each of the six criteria separately (Purpose, Content, Conciseness & Clarity, Genre & Style, Organisation & Layout, Language)Blends everything into one vibe and one number
Checks the letter against the task's case notes — what was expanded that shouldn't be, what was missedJudges the letter as prose, ignoring selection
Flags word-count drift against the 180–200 targetMay not check at all unless asked
Points at the exact line to fixSummarises in generalities

The self-assessment loop (no tutor required)

  1. Write one timed letter from a real task (case notes + letter stimulus).
  2. Get per-criterion feedback — structured, against the six criteria, including case-note selection and word count.
  3. Fix that one letter rather than writing a fresh one badly — rewriting the same letter against the feedback teaches faster than volume.
  4. Track your repeated errors across letters: the slip you make in every letter is worth more than any new practice task.
  5. In the final week: one properly-reviewed letter per day beats five unreviewed ones.

The structured version, pre-built

OET Writing Coach GPT is that structured loop as a tool: paste a practice letter and get a breakdown per criterion — where marks are actually lost, which case-note details were missed or shouldn't be there, and where the letter drifts from the 180–200 word target. It needs a ChatGPT Plus account.
