The ECFMG single-sitting rule: why failing one OET sub-test means retaking all four

If you're an international medical graduate using OET Medicine for ECFMG Pathways, one rule shapes your entire exam strategy — and plenty of candidates find it out the expensive way.

The requirement, verbatim

"Applicants must attain a minimum score of 350 on the Listening, Reading, and Speaking sub-tests, and a minimum score of 300 on the Writing sub-test, in one test administration." — ECFMG, Pathways OET requirement (ecfmg.org, accessed October 2026)

Two numbers matter, and they are not the same: 350 for Listening, Reading and Speaking, but only 300 for Writing. And the phrase doing all the work is "in one test administration" — every sub-test, passed in the same sitting.

What it means when you fail one sub-test

"Applicants who do not attain the minimum score on one or more of the measured sub-tests must retake all OET Medicine sub-tests (Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking) and achieve the minimum score on all sub-tests in a single test administration." — ECFMG, same page

There is no carrying a passing sub-test forward. Score 400 in Listening and Reading, pass Speaking, and get 290 in Writing — you retake all four, and all four must clear their bars in that one new sitting. This is why a writing miss costs so much more than the 10-point gap suggests: the retake re-rolls your strongest sub-tests too.

The practical consequences

  • Writing is the asymmetry. At 300 it has the lowest bar, but it's the sub-test candidates most often miss — and the one where preparation method (structured, per-criterion feedback rather than volume) moves scores fastest.
  • Book the retake only when the weak sub-test is actually fixed. Since every sitting re-tests everything, retaking with the same gap re-rolls four dice hoping one lands differently.
  • Check result release. Scores must be released to ECFMG — confirm the release when you book, not after results arrive.
  • Dates matter. For the 2027 Pathways, qualifying scores must be from tests taken on or after January 1, 2025 — plan backwards from your Pathway application, not forwards from your test date.
  • No retake limit. ECFMG imposes none (scheduling availability aside) — the constraint is time and fees, not policy.

A note on the 290–300 gap

Candidates stuck just under the writing bar (290 is a recurring number) are usually losing marks in one criterion, not everywhere — case-note selection, word-count drift past the 180–200 target, or a late purpose line. Because a retake re-tests all four sub-tests, the cheapest path back is fixing that one leak before booking, not hoping a remark or another sitting fixes it for you.
